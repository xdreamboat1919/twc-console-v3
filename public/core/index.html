Attribution

  
Credit and cause are different questions. The reported number answers who was last, not who was responsible, and on a considered purchase with a long path those two answers diverge substantially.


  

    
The path

    
Set where the touches, the click and the purchase land. The tool shows which impressions receive credit under the selected window and which do not, despite having been part of the same journey.

    

      

      

      

      

    

    
DayTouchInteractionCreditedActual role

    

  


  

    
Where the shared framing is right, and where it needs care

    
Correct, and it is the important part
      Ads one through four influenced the purchase and receive nothing. Attribution is a credit-assignment rule, not a causal model, and judging an introducer on closing metrics is a category error. On a $299 considered purchase with a one to two week fulfilment window, the real consideration path is longer than the seven-day click window can see, so this catalog systematically under-attributes.

    
Needs care: continued spend is evidence, not proof
      Meta does not run a documented ad-level model that assigns assist roles and deliberately keeps an ad alive for its position in a sequence. Ad-level delivery is driven by predicted conversion rate and estimated action rate. So continued spend is weak evidence that the model sees something useful, and it is not confirmation that the ad is doing sequence work.

    
The rule still holds, for a better reason
      Do not kill an ad below the minimum read. Below three to five times target CPA in spend, a zero-conversion ad is statistically indistinguishable from a good ad that got unlucky. That is the defensible reason not to cut early, and it survives scrutiny in a way that an assumed assist model does not.

    
The only real answer to causation is a holdout
      No amount of window adjustment answers what would have happened without the ad. Incrementality testing does. Everything else is a better or worse proxy, and MER is the most useful of them because it is attribution-free.

How to build the survey so it is usable

    
Ask on the thank-you page, not by emailResponse rate roughly doubles and the answer is given while the journey is still fresh. An email sent two days later collects the last thing they remember rather than the thing that worked.

    
Free-text option alongside the listThe pre-set list encodes what you already believe. The free-text field is where you discover the channel nobody knew was working, and on this catalog it is where podcast and personality-driven traffic will surface.

    
Never let the list start with the answer you wantOrder bias is real and large. Randomise the option order per session, or accept that the first option is over-reported by a meaningful margin.

    
Treat it as directional, not as truthPeople misremember, they conflate seeing an ad with searching, and they under-report advertising generally. The value is in the trend and in the gap against platform reporting, not in the absolute number.

    
No health questions, everThe temptation to add "what are you hoping to address" is strong and it converts a marketing survey into health data collection at checkout. It is the wrong data in the wrong place with the wrong consent, on a domain that must stay Class A.

Build notes for this catalog

    
Order bump belongs on the kitA $299 order absorbs a $39 add-on more easily than a $48 order does, and the take rate on high-AOV carts is consistently better. Put a consumable with an obvious relationship to the kit in that slot, not a random SKU.

Membership attach is the highest-value and the hardestA member is worth several times a one-time buyer, so the arithmetic favours it heavily. It also asks for a commitment at the moment of least trust. Test it post-purchase before testing it in-cart.

    
Do not bump on the Rx flowThat checkout already carries a medical intake and a one to two week wait. Adding a decision to a flow with known drop-off between payment and completed intake trades a small AOV gain for a larger fulfilment loss.

What to look for, and what to ignore

    
Longevity above 60 daysPrioritize the mechanism for investigation. Long runtime can indicate durable use, but it may also reflect low spend, evergreen coverage or an unattended ad. Validate the idea with your own test.

    
Claims they sustain that you do notThis is intelligence for the compliance conversation—not evidence of approval, safety, substantiation or permission to copy.

    
Volume of active adsUseful for understanding execution depth and testing cadence, and easy to over-read. Fifty active ads may be variants of two concepts; volume does not reveal budget.

    
Do not copy executionA competitor's winning ad is winning against their offer, their landing page, their price and their audience. Copy the mechanism, meaning why it works, and never the asset. Borrowed execution without the underlying system consistently underperforms.

    
Ads that disappearedWorth more than the ones still running. An aggressive claim that ran and then vanished is a compliance boundary someone else paid to discover.

Automation

  
Automated rules are useful for surveillance and dangerous for action. On a gated catalog a rule that scales spend into a restricted product does damage no dashboard will catch in time.

  

    
Rule library

    

      
      
      
    

    

  

  

    
The governing principle

    
Automate notification, not actionA rule that tells you something changed is always safe. A rule that changes something on your behalf is safe only where the change is reversible, bounded, and cannot touch a gated product. On this account that is a short list, and the temptation to extend it is exactly how an automated scale rule ends up funding a campaign nobody authorised.

    
Rules do not understand learningAn automated pause on a three-day CPA window will kill ads mid-learning, repeatedly, and the account will never stabilise. Any action rule needs a lookback longer than the learning phase and a minimum spend condition, or it optimises against noise faster than a person can.

How to force delivery on a new asset

    
1. Use the testing campaign, not the scaling oneThis is what the separate ABO testing environment exists for. A new asset in a scaling CBO competes against creatives with weeks of delivery history and a trained model, and it will usually lose that competition before it has been read.

    
2. Give each new asset its own ad set during the testGuaranteed spend per cell is the entire reason concept testing uses ABO. Inside a shared ad set the allocation is Meta's decision, not yours, and you cannot force it back.

    
3. Temporarily pause the incumbent, with careIt works and it costs volume while it runs. Do it only when the pipeline has a validated successor ready, and never on the account's only proven asset.

    
4. Cap live creatives at six to eight per ad setBeyond that Meta concentrates on a few and the rest sit dormant. Adding a ninth asset to a crowded ad set is not a test, it is a hope.

    
What does not workDuplicating the ad set to force a reset. It resets learning, costs five to seven days, and frequently produces the same allocation because the underlying prediction has not changed.

Deduplication

    
The single most common fault, and the one that looks like everything else. Running both browser and server means the same purchase arrives twice unless the two are matched.

    

      

        
How to prove it works

        

          
1Open Events Manager, Test Events. Enter the site in test mode.

          
2Complete a real purchase end to end.

          
3You should see the browser event and the server event arrive separately.

          
4Both must carry an identical event_id, and it should be the order ID.

          
5Meta should mark one as deduplicated. If both count, the match failed.

          
6Repeat for AddToCart and InitiateCheckout, not only Purchase.

          
7Check Diagnostics weekly thereafter. Dedup breaks silently on theme and app updates.

        

      

      

        
How it fails

        
Dedup on Purchase onlyPurchase looks clean while AddToCart and InitiateCheckout double count. Every mid-funnel rate is then inflated, and every funnel diagnosis run against them points at the wrong step.

        
event_id generated separately on each sideA timestamp or a random ID will never match. It must be the same value from the same source, which in practice means the order ID.

        
Different event namesA server event named purchase against a browser Purchase will not deduplicate. Name and ID both have to match.

        
Server event arrives outside the windowMeta deduplicates within a limited window. A server event queued and sent hours later may count separately.

        
Thank-you page reloadA customer refreshing the confirmation page fires Purchase again. The order ID as event_id solves this at the same time as it solves dedup.

Make scenarios

  
Meta's native rules can only see Meta. Make sits outside the platform and can read the ad account, Shopify and the CRM in the same run, which is where the useful automation actually lives. It can also act with far less protection than a native rule, so the same discipline applies: automate observation freely, automate action carefully.


  

    
The operating principle

    
Read-only scenarios are close to risk-freeA scenario that pulls insights, compares them against a target and posts to Slack cannot damage anything. Build these freely and build them first, because most of the value is in seeing a problem the same day rather than at the weekly.

    
Write scenarios need the same rules as native automationAnything that changes a budget, pauses an ad or creates a campaign needs a lookback longer than the learning phase, a minimum spend condition, a hard ceiling, and an explicit exclusion for any campaign carrying the gated suffix.

    
One rule that is absolute hereNo Make scenario may increase spend on a campaign whose name ends in _GATED. Build the filter as a blocking condition at the top of the flow rather than as a check inside it, so the scenario cannot proceed at all rather than relying on a branch being written correctly.

    
On Meta modules in MakeMake's native Facebook Ads app covers a fraction of the Marketing API. For reading insights, pulling delivery status and writing budget changes, the reliable path is the HTTP module calling graph.facebook.com directly with a system user token. Slower to build, and it does not break when the app's coverage changes.

ROAS recovery

  
Three passes, in order, when return on ad spend drops. Cost per funnel step tells you where it broke. Breakdowns tell you whether Meta moved your spend somewhere worse. Incremental attribution tells you whether the loss is real demand or credit you were never causing.


  

    
The method

    
1 · Cost per funnel step, not just CPAAdd cost per add to cart, cost per initiated checkout and cost per purchase as columns. CPA on its own says the number got worse. Three costs in sequence say where the cost accumulated, and each step points at a different fix.

    
2 · Every breakdownAge, gender, placement, platform. The question is whether Meta changed where your spend is going. Delivery composition can shift without you touching anything, and a blended number will not show it.

Optimization strategy
Work from the first broken layer, not from the metric that is easiest to change.

  


  

    
The decision hierarchy
Read top to bottom. A failure higher in the list invalidates decisions below it.

    

      
1
Account and compliance
Restrictions, disapprovals, destination policy, authorization, claim approval. Protect delivery before optimizing it.

      
2
Measurement integrity
Pixel/CAPI health, deduplication, event timing, backend reconciliation, valid orders, refunds, and attribution lag.

      
3
Delivery and pacing
Budget utilization, learning state, bid constraints, audience size, auction overlap, frequency, and schedule.

      
4
Funnel mechanics
CPM → hook → click → landing-page view → add to cart → checkout → valid order.

      
5
Commercial economics
New-customer CPA, acquisition ROAS, contribution after media, payback, refund rate, and marginal efficiency.

      
6
Controlled action
Change one material lever, write the expected result and rollback boundary, then protect the read window.

    

    
Never scale through a broken layerMore budget amplifies whatever is currently limiting the system. If tracking is wrong, the algorithm learns from the wrong signal. If checkout leaks, creative buys more leakage. If compliance is unclear, scale multiplies account risk.

Forecasting and scale economics
A forecast is a range with assumptions and a rollback rule, never a promise.

  


  

    
Forecast packet
No budget proposal is complete until every required input has a source and reporting window.

    

      
BaselineCurrent daily budgetPlus actual spend, valid orders, backend revenue, new customers, refunds, and stable read days.

      
TargetsCPA, ROAS, contributionDeclare the binding target. Include contribution per order, payback limit, and cash requirement.

      
IncrementProposed budget and datesAmount, percentage change, method, launch date, cooldown, recheck, and owner.

      
ResponseElasticity and marginal resultUse the last comparable budget steps to estimate how orders and efficiency responded.

      
CapacityCreative and audience headroomValidated assets, spend concentration, frequency, reach, winning lifespan, and replacements due.

      
OperationsInventory and cash flowInventory cover, fulfilment capacity, refund exposure, payment timing, and compliance status.

Budget change(proposed budget ÷ current budget) − 1

      
Scenario daily ordersbaseline × [1 + (budget change × elasticity)]

      
Scenario CPAproposed daily spend ÷ scenario daily orders

      
Contribution after media(orders × contribution/order) − spend

      
Forecast error(actual − forecast) ÷ forecast

Replace them with the median response from your own comparable scale steps as history accumulates.

Scale-readiness gate
All eight gates must be checked. No single ROAS screenshot overrides a failed gate.

  


  

    
Eight pieces of evidence
These match the live Scale control room so the manual and the decision tool use one language.

    

      
01Data
Backend-valid orders and revenue reconciled; event health, deduplication, timing, and attribution lag understood.
Blocker: broken or unexplained signal.

      
02Economics
CPA or ROAS clears the declared target using commercial truth; contribution and payback remain viable.
Blocker: average economics fail.

      
03Stability
A representative read window clears the minimum evidence threshold without a confounding promotion or outage.
Blocker: noise, launch volatility, or mixed windows.

      
04Marginal
The last additional dollar cleared the guardrail. Use the last increment, not lifetime blended performance.
Blocker: marginal CPA/ROAS fails or is unknown.

      
05Creative depth
At least two validated live assets or one winner plus a ready replacement; no single point of fatigue failure.
Blocker: one-creative dependency.

      
06Delivery
Stable state, sufficient eligible reach, manageable frequency, and no unresolved audience or bid constraint.
Blocker: learning, saturation, or delivery fault.

      
07Operations
Inventory, fulfilment, cash flow, customer support, and refund exposure can absorb the forecasted volume.
Blocker: less capacity than the campaign can create.

      
08Compliance
Creative, claim, disclaimer, destination, product class, authorization, and next review date are current.
Blocker: any unresolved policy or legal review.

The metric stack

    
CPA is a summary of things that each fail differently. Read down the stack, not at the bottom of it.

    

      
LayerMetricAnswersWhen it alone misleads

      
        
AuctionCPMWhat impressions costRising CPM with flat CTR is cost, not performance. Cutting budget here removes reach you were converting.

        
AttentionHook rate, 3s / impressionsWhether the creative stopped anyoneNothing downstream is readable until this clears, because the sample below it is already filtered.

        
InterestCTR, linkWhether attention became intentHigh CTR with weak conversion usually means the click was bought with a promise the page does not keep.

        
ArrivalLanding page view rateWhether clicks became sessionsBelow 65% this is page speed or a broken link, and no creative or bidding change touches it.

        
ConsiderationCost per add to cartWhat a qualified visitor costsThe single most useful number nobody adds. At benchmark with a bad CPA, the creative worked and something downstream did not.

        
IntentCost per checkout initiatedWhat a committed visitor costsRising here with cost per add to cart flat isolates the product page and the offer.

        
ConversionCost per purchaseWhat a customer costsA summary. On its own it says the number moved and never where.

        
ValueROAS, AOVWhat the customer is worthFlattered by returning revenue and renewals. Pair with acquisition ROAS.

        
BusinessMER, nCACWhether the business is growingOnly honest when new and returning are separated.

      
    

    
The three bold rows are the ones most accounts never addCost per add to cart, cost per checkout initiated and cost per purchase read in sequence turn a CPA that got worse into a specific step that broke. Each step points at a different fix and three of them are not media fixes at all. Add these as a saved column set in Ads Manager once, and every future diagnosis starts two hours ahead.

Glossary

    
TermMeaning here

      
Learning capacityWhether the selected optimization event produces enough stable signal for the current number of campaigns and ad sets. Judge it from current delivery diagnostics and account history, not one universal conversion number.

      
Marginal CPACost of the additional conversions from the last budget increment. Blended CPA hides decay; this does not.

      
Marginal ROASAdditional revenue attributed to the additional spend from the latest comparable increment. It is the return on the next dollars, not the historical average.

      
MERTotal business revenue divided by total marketing spend. Business-level truth, unlike platform ROAS.

      
Minimum readThe predeclared evidence required before a creative decision. The console starts at three to five times target CPA in spend, then expects you to replace that heuristic with account-specific conversion and variance history.

      
ElasticityHow strongly valid-order volume responds to a budget change. Estimate it from comparable scale steps and use a range in forecasts.

      
ControlThe proven baseline kept stable so a new challenger can be evaluated against a known operating condition.

      
ChallengerA validated alternative seeking a larger share of delivery. It earns scale by beating the declared decision rule, not by having the highest CTR.

      
Creative depthThe number and diversity of validated assets able to carry spend without one winner becoming a single point of failure.

      
Scale ceilingThe spend level where the next increment no longer clears marginal economics, delivery, creative, or operational capacity.

      
Rollback thresholdThe metric value or risk event written before launch that requires a return to the previous known-good state.

      
Spend concentrationThe share of campaign or portfolio spend carried by one asset, concept, audience, or product. High concentration increases fatigue and continuity risk.

      
Class A / Class BCommerce data and clinical data. Different measurement stacks, always.

      
GatedProduct needing LegitScript certification plus separate written Meta authorization.

      
Approved claim referenceThe specific permitted claim a creative is built on. Makes compliance auditable at asset level.

      
Payback periodMonths for cumulative contribution to recover CAC. Governs cash, where the ratio governs profit.

      
ConcentrationTop tier revenue share divided by its customer share. Above 2.5 means a value-based lookalike is worth building.
